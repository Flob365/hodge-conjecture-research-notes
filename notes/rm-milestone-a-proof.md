# Milestone A: audited status and the two-torsion exception

> **Status.** The previous version claimed rank-10 injectivity of the twisted Kodaira--Spencer map for every nontrivial degree-zero twist. That claim was too strong. The Wronskian formula is correct only after choosing a covariant horizontal trivialization, and at nonzero two-torsion the covariant correction creates genuine extra kernel directions. Numerical theta-function calculations on a generic smooth ppav fourfold consistently give rank `10` for generic non-torsion twists but rank `7` at nonzero two-torsion. The generic rank-10 statement remains a strong target; the two-torsion rank-7 statement is presently an experimentally verified structural conjecture, not yet a theorem. The original construction only needs rank at least `3`.

## 1. Intrinsic map

Let `(X,Theta)` be a principally polarized complex abelian fourfold with smooth theta divisor

\[
D=\Theta,
\qquad K_D=O_D(\Theta).
\]

For nontrivial `P in Pic^0(X)`, let

\[
s_P\in H^0(D,K_D\otimes P^{-1})
\]

be its unique nonzero twisted canonical section. Contraction gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho_P:H^1(T_D)_{ppav}
\longrightarrow
H^1(\Omega_D^2\otimes P^{-1}),
}
\]

with dimensions

\[
10\longrightarrow11.
\]

Milestone A requires only

\[
rank(\rho_P)\ge3.
\]

## 2. Covariant theta representative

Choose a theta equation `theta(z,tau)` and a horizontal theta section `theta_P` representing the flat twist `P`. A period deformation is represented by a symmetric tensor

\[
Q\in Sym^2\mathbf C^4.
\]

The heat equation converts the period derivative into second `z`-derivatives. The global section representing `rho_P(Q)` is not, in a fixed translation chart, simply `theta_P tr(Q Hess theta)`: second derivatives of theta have inhomogeneous automorphy terms.

After covariant correction, the section on `D` can be represented by the transvectant-type expression

\[
\boxed{
U_{Q,P}
=
\theta_P\,\operatorname{tr}(Q\,Hess\,\theta)
-2(\nabla\theta)^tQ\nabla\theta_P,
}
\]

up to the convention-dependent overall heat-equation factor and addition of the four gauge sections

\[
\theta_P\theta_i|_D.
\]

Equivalently, in the global `2Theta tensor P^{-1}` picture one can package the same class by the variation Wronskian

\[
F_Q=\theta\,\delta_Q\theta_P-\theta_P\,\delta_Q\theta
\]

modulo the five-dimensional gauge space generated by `theta theta_P` and the four first-order Wronskians.

The important correction to the previous proof is that the horizontal derivative of `theta_P` cannot in general be replaced by an ordinary derivative of the translated function with all flat data held naively fixed. This matters precisely on special torsion loci.

## 3. What the numerical audit shows

Using direct truncated theta series on a generic indecomposable period matrix, solving numerically for points of `Theta`, and evaluating the covariant section `U_{Q,P}` modulo the four gauge directions gives a very stable singular-value pattern.

### Generic non-torsion `P`

For several generic twists the ten images are independent:

\[
\boxed{rank(\rho_P)=10\quad\text{numerically}.}
\]

The smallest singular value is separated from numerical truncation noise by many orders of magnitude.

### Nonzero two-torsion `P`

For several even and odd nonzero two-torsion characteristics the rank drops to

\[
\boxed{rank(\rho_P)=7\quad\text{numerically}.}
\]

Three singular values fall to the theta-series truncation/noise level while seven remain stable.

This is not a small numerical perturbation of rank ten; it is a representation-theoretic-looking rank drop.

## 4. Why two-torsion is structurally special

If `P=P^{-1}`, the line bundle

\[
2\Theta\otimes P
\]

is symmetric under `[-1]`. For nonzero two-torsion, its 16-dimensional section space splits into two inversion eigenspaces. The five-dimensional gauge package has mixed parity:

- `theta theta_P` has the parity of `theta_P`;
- each first Wronskian
  \[
  W_i=\theta(\theta_P)_i-\theta_P\theta_i
  \]
  has the opposite parity.

The covariant period-variation class has a fixed parity. After quotienting by the five gauge directions, the eleven-dimensional twisted Hodge space consequently acquires a nontrivial parity decomposition. The numerical rank-seven image is consistent with one of these eigenspaces being seven-dimensional.

A rigorous proof now needs only standard theta-group/inversion character calculations plus surjectivity onto the relevant eigenspace.

## 5. Consequence for the research program

The previous universal statement

\[
rank(\rho_P)=10\quad\forall P\ne O
\]

is withdrawn.

What survives:

1. the intrinsic `10 -> 11` map and its theta/Jacobian description;
2. strong evidence for generic rank ten away from special torsion loci;
3. strong evidence for rank seven at nonzero two-torsion;
4. Milestone A itself, which needs only rank at least three, is compatible with both regimes.

The two-torsion point is in fact now preferred for Milestone B because Serre duality identifies the central `11 x 11` IVHS block with a symmetric bilinear form and the rank-seven outer image creates a useful `7+4` decomposition.

## 6. New exact target

Prove the **two-torsion rank lemma**:

> For a general smooth principal theta divisor in dimension four and a suitable nonzero two-torsion `P`,
> \[
> \boxed{rank(\rho_P)=7.}
> \]

A convenient proof strategy is:

1. compute the `[-1]` eigenspace dimensions of `H^0(2Theta tensor P)` via the theta group / holomorphic Lefschetz formula;
2. track the parities of `theta theta_P` and the four Wronskians;
3. show the covariant heat-equation image lands in the resulting seven-dimensional quotient eigenspace;
4. prove it spans that eigenspace at one point, hence generically by semicontinuity.

This replaces the invalid universal-injectivity claim by a sharper and more useful torsion statement.
