# A local no-go theorem for the RM elementary-transform ansatz

> **Status.** This note proves that the coherent elementary-transform construction in `rm-positive-curve-elementary-transform.md` cannot kill both mixed RM Atiyah directions on a simple RM abelian fourfold. The obstruction is local and survives before semiregularity is applied. The positive cubic identity and coherent K-class realization remain valid; what fails is the final generalized deformation property.

## 1. Local characteristic morphism for the basic ideal

Keep the completed local model

\[
R=k[[w,x,y,z]],
\qquad
D:(w=0),
\qquad
Z:(w=y=z=0),
\]

and

\[
I=(y,z)\subset R/(w).
\]

From `rm-elementary-transform-local-ext.md`, a minimal free resolution is

\[
0\to R
\xrightarrow{d_2}R^3
\xrightarrow{d_1}R^2
\to I\to0,
\]

with

\[
d_2=
\begin{pmatrix}w\\z\\-y\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 d_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
-z&w&0\\
y&0&w
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Using the trivial connections on the free modules, the Atiyah class is represented by the exterior differentials of the matrices. The degree-two component of its square is the path

\[
R\xrightarrow{d(d_2)}R^3\otimes\Omega^1_R
\xrightarrow{d(d_1)}R^2\otimes\Omega^2_R
\to I\otimes\Omega^2_R.
\]

A direct multiplication gives

\[
d(d_1)\wedge d(d_2)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2\,dw\wedge dz\\
-2\,dw\wedge dy
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

After the final map `(y,z)`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac12 at_I^2
=dw\wedge(y\,dz-z\,dy)
\quad\text{in}\quad
(I/I^2)\otimes\Omega_X^2.
}
\]

Recall

\[
Ext^2_R(I,I)\cong I/I^2
\]

with basis `[y],[z]` over `O_Z`.

## 2. Bivector obstruction formula

Let `P` be a holomorphic bivector. Contracting the displayed two-form yields the local degree-two characteristic/Atiyah obstruction

\[
\boxed{
ob_I(P)
=[y]\,P(dw,dz)-[z]\,P(dw,dy)
\in I/I^2.
}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
ob_I(P)=0
\iff
P(dw,dy)=P(dw,dz)=0
\quad\text{along }Z.
}
\]

This is the exact local falsification criterion for a bivector direction.

A global `B`-field class in `H^2(O_X)` has zero image under restriction to the stalkwise edge term `H^0(mathcal Ext^2)`: it is locally split on an affine/complete-local neighborhood and enters total `Ext^2` through the global-cohomology filtration. Therefore the local edge projection of a mixed class

\[
B-q^{-1}P
\]

is just the nonzero scalar multiple of the bivector expression above.

Hence a nonzero local bivector obstruction cannot be cancelled by the `B`-field part.

## 3. Apply the criterion to the RM splitting

Write the holomorphic cotangent space as

\[
V^*=U_+^*\oplus U_-^*,
\qquad
\dim U_+^*=\dim U_-^*=2.
\]

Let

\[
P_+\in\wedge^2U_+,
\qquad
P_-\in\wedge^2U_-
\]

be the two nondegenerate bivectors appearing in the RM kernel.

At a point of `Z`, decompose the three conormal covectors as

\[
dw=a_++a_-,
\qquad
dy=b_++b_-,
\qquad
dz=c_++c_-.
\]

Suppose both local bivector obstructions vanish. Then

\[
P_+(a_+,b_+)=P_+(a_+,c_+)=0
\]

and

\[
P_-(a_-,b_-)=P_-(a_-,c_-)=0.
\]

Because a nonzero alternating form on a two-dimensional space is nondegenerate, if `a_+ != 0` then both `b_+` and `c_+` are proportional to `a_+`. Likewise, if `a_- != 0`, both `b_-` and `c_-` are proportional to `a_-`.

If both `a_+` and `a_-` are nonzero, the three covectors `dw,dy,dz` all lie in

\[
\operatorname{span}\{a_+,a_-\},
\]

which has dimension at most two. This contradicts the assumption that `D,L_1,L_2` meet transversely along the smooth codimension-three curve `Z`.

Therefore at every point of `Z`, at least one of `a_+,a_-` is zero.

## 4. Irreducibility forces tangency to one RM distribution

Assume `Z` is irreducible. The conditions

\[
a_+=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a_-=0
\]

are closed analytic conditions along `Z`. Their union is all of `Z`. Hence one of them holds identically.

Suppose, for example,

\[
a_+=0
\quad\text{along }Z.
\]

Then `a_-` is nonzero and the vanishing of the `P_-` obstruction forces `b_-` and `c_-` to be multiples of `a_-`. Subtracting multiples of `dw` from `dy,dz`, the remaining two conormals lie entirely in `U_+^*`. Transversality forces them to span `U_+^*`.

Thus the tangent line of `Z` is contained in the fixed distribution

\[
T_Z\subset U_-\otimes O_Z.
\]

The alternative case gives

\[
T_Z\subset U_+\otimes O_Z.
\]

## 5. Simplicity rules out such a curve

Let `B_Z` be the smallest abelian subvariety generated by the differences `Z-Z`. Its tangent space is generated by translates of tangent directions to `Z`.

If

\[
T_Z\subset U_-\otimes O_Z,
\]

then

\[
T_0B_Z\subseteq U_-
\]

and hence

\[
\dim B_Z\le2.
\]

Since `Z` is nonconstant, `B_Z` is positive dimensional. Therefore `B_Z` is a proper nonzero abelian subvariety of the fourfold.

On a **simple** abelian fourfold this is impossible.

The same argument applies to `U_+`.

We conclude:

\[
\boxed{
\text{on a simple RM abelian fourfold, no transverse correcting curve }Z
\text{ can have both }ob_I(P_+)=ob_I(P_-)=0.
}
\]

## 6. Consequence for the two mixed directions

The two generalized Chern-kernel vectors are

\[
\xi_+=B_+-q^{-1}P_+,
\qquad
\xi_-=B_--q^{-1}P_-.
\]

The `B`-field parts have zero stalkwise degree-two edge projection, while the bivector parts are detected by the formula of Section 2.

Therefore, on every correcting curve in the elementary-transform model, at least one of the two mixed Atiyah classes has nonzero local edge projection. In particular the global sheaf cannot satisfy

\[
\xi_+,\xi_-\in\ker(at_E)
\]

simultaneously.

Thus the construction from `rm-positive-curve-elementary-transform.md` is rejected as a solution of the final semiregularity problem on a simple RM fourfold.

## 7. What survives

Several useful pieces remain valid:

1. the positive cubic identity
   \[
   \Delta C^3=3(T-2)AC^2+3AH^2
   \]
   is exact and gives a positive effective realization of the correcting class;
2. the resulting elementary transform is a coherent representative of an integral multiple of the RM secant class;
3. the local Ext calculation reduces its failure to a transparent conormal obstruction.

The no-go theorem tells us what the next coupling **must not** look like:

> a successful RM object cannot obtain its cubic correction solely by ordinary elementary transforms along smooth transverse curves inside one divisor.

To evade the obstruction, the coupling must alter the local derived geometry itself -- for example by using a nontrivial two-term complex on overlapping support, a nonreduced/derived intersection, or a Fourier--Mukai transform in which the two bivector channels are mixed before taking the Atiyah obstruction.
