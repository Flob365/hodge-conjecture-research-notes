# Local Ext algebra of the RM elementary transform

> **Status.** This note computes the completed local self-Ext algebra of the basic elementary-transform summand appearing in `rm-positive-curve-elementary-transform.md`. The calculation shows that its intrinsic degree-two obstruction sheaf is rank two on the correcting curve. This matches the number of unresolved generalized RM directions, but does not by itself prove that their Atiyah classes vanish.

## 1. Local model

Let

\[
R=k[[w,x,y,z]]
\]

be the completed local ring of the ambient fourfold. Let the smooth divisor and correcting curve be

\[
D:(w=0),
\qquad
Z:(w=y=z=0).
\]

Put

\[
A=R/(w)\cong k[[x,y,z]],
\qquad
B=A/(y,z)\cong k[[x]].
\]

The kernel of the basic quotient

\[
A\twoheadrightarrow B
\]

is

\[
I=(y,z)\subset A.
\]

Thus, after trivializing the divisor bundle and the curve quotient, every elementary transform is locally a direct sum of copies of `I` and free `A`-summands.

## 2. A minimal free resolution over the fourfold

As an `R`-module, `I` is generated by `y,z`. The relations are

\[
(-z,y),\qquad(w,0),\qquad(0,w).
\]

They have the single second syzygy

\[
(w,z,-y).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
0\longrightarrow R
\xrightarrow{d_2}R^3
\xrightarrow{d_1}R^2
\xrightarrow{d_0}I
\longrightarrow0,
}
\]

with

\[
d_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
w\\ z\\-y
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 d_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
-z&w&0\\
y&0&w
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 d_0=(y\ z).
\]

The compositions vanish immediately.

Exactness can also be certified homologically. Over

\[
A=k[[x,y,z]],
\]

the exact sequence

\[
0\to I\to A\to B\to0
\]

gives `depth_A(I)=2`. Since `w` annihilates `I`, `depth_R(I)=2`. As `R` is regular of dimension four, Auslander--Buchsbaum gives

\[
\operatorname{pd}_R(I)=2,
\]

so the displayed length-two free resolution has the expected projective dimension.

## 3. Self-Ext calculation

Apply `Hom_R(-,I)`. Because `wI=0`, the resulting cochain complex is

\[
I^2\xrightarrow{\delta_0}I^3\xrightarrow{\delta_1}I,
\]

where

\[
\delta_0(a,b)=(-za+yb,0,0)
\]

and

\[
\delta_1(c_1,c_2,c_3)=zc_2-yc_3.
\]

The degree-two group is therefore

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^2_R(I,I)
=I/(yI+zI)
=I/I^2.
\]

Since `I/I^2` is the conormal bundle of `Z` in `D`, locally free of rank two over `B`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Ext}^2_R(I,I)
\cong B^{\oplus2}.
}
\]

The two generators are represented by the conormal classes of `y` and `z`.

The lower groups are equally explicit. The equation

\[
zc_2=yc_3
\]

forces

\[
c_2=yh,\qquad c_3=zh
\]

for a unique `h in A`, while

\[
\operatorname{im}(\delta_0)=I^2\oplus0\oplus0.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hom}_R(I,I)\cong A,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(I,I)
\cong A\oplus I/I^2.
}
\]

The `A` summand is naturally associated with moving the ambient divisor branch; the `I/I^2` summand records first-order deformation of the curve inside the divisor.

## 4. Cross terms with an unmodified divisor summand

A free divisor summand is the `R`-module

\[
A=R/(w).
\]

It has projective dimension one, hence

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^{\ge2}_R(A,I)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ext}^{\ge2}_R(A,A)=0.
\]

Applying `Hom_R(-,A)` to the resolution of `I` gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Ext}^2_R(I,A)
\cong A/(y,z)
\cong B.
}
\]

Thus an elementary transform of a rank-`r` free divisor bundle by a rank-`s` quotient on `Z` can be reduced locally to

\[
I^{\oplus s}\oplus A^{\oplus(r-s)},
\]

and every degree-two local obstruction is supported on `Z`.

## 5. Why rank two is significant

The exact Chern-action calculation for the RM secant class leaves precisely two generalized kernel directions,

\[
\xi_+=B_+-q^{-1}P_+,
\qquad
\xi_-=B_--q^{-1}P_-.
\]

The intrinsic degree-two obstruction space of one modified rank-one divisor summand is likewise the rank-two conormal module

\[
I/I^2=N^*_{Z/D}.
\]

This numerical agreement is not a proof of cancellation, but it shows that the remaining problem is not hiding in an uncontrolled high-rank local obstruction group. The elementary transform has exactly two basic local obstruction channels normal to the curve inside `D`.

## 6. The local-to-global shape of the remaining calculation

For the global elementary-transform sheaf `E`, the local-to-global spectral sequence

\[
E_2^{p,q}=H^p(\mathcal Ext^q_X(E,E))
\Longrightarrow Ext^{p+q}_X(E,E)
\]

shows why a purely affine stalk calculation cannot settle the mixed `B/P` directions:

- the `B`-field part originates from global `H^2(O_X)` data;
- the bivector part has a local normal/conormal contribution through the Atiyah class of the support and the elementary transform;
- their equality or cancellation is a global statement in total `Ext^2(E,E)`.

The local computation nevertheless identifies the only new degree-two sheaf-level channel created by modifying a rank-one summand: `N^*_{Z/D}`.

## 7. Sharpened next target

For each correcting curve, compute the projection of the two Atiyah obstruction classes

\[
at_E(\xi_+),\qquad at_E(\xi_-)
\]

to the local conormal quotient

\[
\mathcal Ext^2_X(E,E)|_Z\twoheadrightarrow N^*_{Z/D}.
\]

There are then only two possibilities:

1. both projections vanish identically; the remaining problem is purely global and can be attacked through the local-to-global filtration;
2. one projection is nonzero; the current elementary-transform model is rejected immediately, because no global `B`-field class can erase a nonzero associated-graded local obstruction.

This gives a concrete falsification test before any full global Ext computation.
